      How it works

      Each city gets a "typical year": twelve monthly averages of daily highs and lows, dew point (how muggy it feels), rain, rainy days, cloud cover, snowfall, wind, and how much temperatures swing from day to day. These come from 2016–2025 hourly weather data. Cities are compared month by month. Temperature counts most, then humidity, rain and sky. Use What matters to you? above to change that. The match percentage is a friendly score: 100% would be an identical climate.

      Temperature, humidity, cloud and wind come from the ERA5 and ERA5-Land reanalysis (Copernicus, via Open-Meteo open data). Rainfall is corrected against nearby rain gauges (NOAA GHCN-Daily and GSOD). Where no reliable gauge exists, it is scaled to WorldClim 2.1 normals. Reanalysis smooths over very local climates, like San Francisco's fog belt or a city's hillside suburbs.

      The region map uses NOAA's climate divisions, which group each state into areas with similar climate. Each division's weather is measured at the largest town in its central part, so a city on its edge, like Manhattan in the Hudson Valley division, doesn't speak for the whole area.
